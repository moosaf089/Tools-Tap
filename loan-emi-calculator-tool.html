What Is an EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment. When you take a loan, you repay it in fixed monthly payments. Each EMI has two parts: the principal (the money you borrowed) and the interest (the cost of borrowing). Early in the loan, most of your EMI goes toward interest; later, more of it goes toward principal.

How the Loan EMI Calculator Works

Our calculator uses the standard reducing-balance EMI formula:

EMI = P × r × (1 + r)n / ((1 + r)n − 1)

Where:

  • P is the loan amount (principal).
  • r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100).
  • n is the total number of monthly installments (tenure in years × 12).

If the interest rate is 0%, the EMI equals the principal divided by the number of months.

How to Use the Calculator

Understanding the Amortization Schedule

The schedule shows how each monthly payment is split between principal and interest, and how the remaining balance decreases over the life of the loan. The final payment is adjusted so the balance reaches exactly zero, avoiding small rounding leftovers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an EMI?

EMI stands for Equated Monthly Installment. It is the fixed amount you pay every month to repay a loan, and it includes both the principal and the interest.

How is the loan EMI calculated?

The calculator uses the standard reducing-balance EMI formula: EMI = P × r × (1+r)n / ((1+r)n − 1), where P is the principal, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12 and then by 100), and n is the total number of monthly installments.

What happens if the interest rate is 0%?

If the interest rate is 0%, the EMI equals the loan amount divided by the number of months. The calculator handles this case correctly without producing an error.

Does the calculator show an amortization schedule?

Yes. After you calculate, you can view a full yearly or monthly amortization schedule showing the payment number, payment amount, principal paid, interest paid, and remaining balance for the entire loan tenure.

Does the final payment leave a remaining balance?

No. The final scheduled payment is adjusted so the remaining balance becomes exactly zero, which prevents small rounding leftovers.
